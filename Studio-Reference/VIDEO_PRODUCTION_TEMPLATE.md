# Video production checklist and template

**v1.5 · 2026-11-19**

Copy this checklist into the scene folder under `Scenes/` when starting a video. Record the template version in the copy. Use one shot plan and one take record per shot or variant. Link existing briefs, prompts, settings and reviews where they already answer a field. Use **Pass / Rework / Pending / N/A** for review checks and note why a check is pending or inapplicable.

Use the [Nocturnal Dream skill](Skills/nocturnal-dream/SKILL.md) to find the relevant chapters, the [Project Instructions](PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS_v1.1.md) for workflow, and the [Scene First Workbook](Scene_First_Workbook_v1.1.xlsx) for planning and tracking. This template is the working checklist for a specific video.

## 1. Scene brief

- [ ] Fill in **working title / scene ID / template version / stage / intended format / duration / budget**. Let the visual idea determine the duration.
- [ ] Write the Altered Reality premise. Name at least one specific cultural anchor, its connection to the intended Douyin audience, and how the viewer will recognize it through image or sound. Record what changes, its rules and the emotion the change could invoke.
- [ ] Choose scale and time cues. Record where they appear and when the viewer can read them. A known landmark, architectural proportions or sound can establish scale. Add signs of everyday use where they serve the scene.
- [ ] Plan the visual discovery in order. Record what the viewer sees first, what the image reveals as it develops, and what deserves time to register.
- [ ] Consider Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value and Stories. Pick two or three and describe the moment or detail that could earn each response. Treat these as audience hypotheses.
- [ ] Gather references and assign each a job such as geography, scale, texture, motion, lighting or identity. Record sources and permissions as applicable.
- [ ] Apply the Creative Bible's Altered Reality test. Check what the scene would lose if its cultural anchor were replaced and whether the alteration and emotional relationship read through image or sound. Review the written direction with the user and record their approval before generation.

## 2. Art direction and shot plan

- [ ] Name the main style preset and intended mood. Describe how the six Visual DNA tenets show up in the image.
  - **Moody Darkness.** Identify shadow detail that must survive at phone size, the motivated light source, its location, falloff and highlight limit.
  - **Dream Haze.** Define atmosphere by depth. Name the key edges and textures that stay legible.
  - **Editorial Surreal Accent.** Name the dominant visual idea and the details that support it. Use an anomaly when the scene calls for one.
  - **Controlled Color Palette.** Record the base, secondary color and optional accent, and where each appears.
  - **Human-Centered Intimacy / Human Scale.** Show readable scale or an emotional point of contact. A landmark, architectural proportion, sound, gesture, or trace of use can serve this role.
  - **Elegant Unreality.** Plan how the unusual element affects contact, shadows, reflections and surrounding materials.
- [ ] Map the space. Sketch or describe the foreground, middle distance and background, their relative sizes, and where major objects sit. Mark the camera path, light positions, sightlines and occlusions. For a real location, verify landmark order and relative placement against a reference.
- [ ] Describe the camera at the **start → action or reveal → end**. Record height, viewing direction, lens or field of view, focus, movement direction and pace, and where it settles. For walking POV, state the path of the feet and the direction of the gaze. Specify how nearby and distant landmarks should travel on screen.
- [ ] Mark where the eye lands in the opening, what the next beat reveals, and what the final hold gives the viewer time to feel or read. Name the specific relationship that makes the scene worth watching, the cue that moves attention, and what would be lost if each shot or pause were cut.
- [ ] List the critical objects and geometry using the table below. Preserve anchor landmarks even when the camera loses sight of them. For adjacent shots, also record match points for screen direction, position, identity, lighting and state.
- [ ] Choose the hardest physical interaction and specify the visible evidence before, during and after it. For a boot in snow, check heel/toe contact, weight and compression, displaced powder and whether a new print remains in place.
- [ ] Plan sound perspective if applicable. Note close action, ambient bed, distant sources and where the perspective shifts. Give music a specific purpose and entry point if used. A picture-only review can mark audio **Pending**.
- [ ] Match continuity preparation to the shot. For a recognizable interface, prop or place, choose a verified identity reference and decide whether to composite the exact asset. For a reveal, block the decisive frame at delivery size with both sides of the relationship visible. Use time-coded rough frames when a state change carries the idea. If a moving camera exposes new surfaces or recurring objects must match across angles, consider a turnaround or a simple 3D scene with a fixed camera path. Record which reference actually controls each element and how the final edit will preserve it.
- [ ] Choose the simplest useful test. State one question, what stays fixed and what would count as a pass. When feasible, establish scale, framing and light with a still before testing movement.

| Anchor or object | Fixed features and location | Allowed movement or state change | Where to verify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Foreground object] | [Shape, count, spacing, markings, attachment] | [Parallax, occlusion, interaction] | [Before, during and after the move] |
| [Background landmark or structure] | [Silhouette, proportions, relative order, horizon] | [Expected screen travel with camera] | [Entry, reveal, final hold] |
| [Character, prop or material state] | [Identity, wardrobe, grip or starting state] | [Action and its lasting effect] | [Contact, exit, re-entry or next shot] |

- [ ] **Motion-ready first frame.** Before video spend, check that the payoff is visible or one short camera move away, that any end frame is reachable from the first frame by the described move, and that something keeps moving when the subject stops.

## 3. Generation and take record

- [ ] Before consuming credits, tokens or limited generation allowance, show the model, output count and quoted cost or spending cap. Record explicit user approval against those settings before submitting.

- [ ] Assign a take ID. Save the exact submitted prompt and any prepared prompt separately. List each input and its actual role, such as image reference, first frame or end frame. Put the shot action in order and name the most important fixed geometry and contact behavior.
- [ ] Record the provider, model/version, mode, job ID, seed if exposed, requested duration/aspect/resolution, audio setting, generation count and actual credits if known. Mark unverified settings explicitly. Log the observed export size, frame rate and duration separately.
- [ ] Keep the raw output with the scene and preserve earlier takes. Link the prompt, references and render in the take record. Document the variable tested and any other settings that changed so the comparison can be interpreted.

## 4. Review and finish

Watch at normal speed, muted, at phone size. Sample the beginning, main action, reveal and last frame. Step frame by frame around contact, occlusion or a suspicious transition. For each issue, record **timecode → visible symptom → impact → possible fix**. Keep observations distinct from inferred causes and creative judgments.

- [ ] **Aesthetic and readability.** The main subject reads immediately when intended. Check color balance, practical highlights, shadow detail, haze, material texture and unwanted exposure shifts.
- [ ] **Detail and scale.** Place cues stay recognizable. Surface detail and traces of use remain attached to their objects. The same world rule has visible consequences across the space where relevant.
- [ ] **Camera and pacing.** The path and gaze read as intended at speed. Check parallax, speed, shake, blur and focus. Watch for sideways drift in a forward move, an apparent zoom, a hidden dissolve and an opening or ending that runs long.
- [ ] **Visual discovery.** At phone size, freeze the first frame and identify the intended focal point. Watch muted and without titles to check that the eye reaches the reveal and that the defining relationship reads. Compare a shorter cut if an opening or hold contributes little. Then check the sound and music on the phone speaker.
- [ ] **Realism and contact.** Check anatomy and gait where applicable, grip, weight transfer, surface response, weather by depth, and whether shadows and reflections follow the moving objects and light.
- [ ] **Object persistence.** Follow each critical anchor through motion and occlusion. Check identity, count, markings and state. Look for duplication, disappearance, sliding texture or an object returning in a different state without a cause.
- [ ] **Geometry.** Compare landmark order, spacing, relative scale and horizon through the shot. Watch for warped lines, changing proportions, drifting ground, floating attachments and structures rearranging at the reveal.
- [ ] **Sound and edit.** Where applicable, review contact sync, ambience, distance, transitions and mix separately. Try local edits first where an issue is confined to timing or a few frames. Compare the edited take against the baseline at normal speed.
- [ ] **Export.** Verify the exported file plays through, framing and safe areas hold at target size, and any titles, captions, color and audio settings carry through. Archive the selected version and link it from the review.
- [ ] **Decision.** Record the selected take, remaining issues and next action. Keep production status and publication status explicit. When publication is in scope, apply the Creative Bible's QC and rights checks, verify the publish package and record audience metrics after release. Record a publication hold when the studio is keeping the product private.

## 5. Improve the checklist after each product

In the scene review, capture **what to repeat / what failed / next controlled test / proposed checklist change**. Link the take and timecodes. Keep one-off shot choices in their scene. Bring a finding into this shared template when it gives future productions a concrete, repeatable check. Record the template version, date, change and supporting scene in the changelog.

### First baseline

[Shanghai After the Snow, take 07](../Scenes/Shanghai-After-the-Snow/REVIEW.md#take-07--walking-pov-picture-review) made the forward walk and gaze turn legible, then held on Pudong long enough to connect the buried foreground rail with the snow across the river. The boot contact and bright lantern exposed two review gaps. Test for a visible material response after foot contact and check whether a practical steals attention at the reveal. For the next edit comparison, hold the end fixed and test whether trimming roughly the first second improves the pace while leaving a readable step.
