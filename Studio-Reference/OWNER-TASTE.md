# Owner taste rules

These are the owner's verdicts on past work, distilled into rules every agent can read: Claude, Gemini in Antigravity, and Codex. Each rule names the round that taught it. Read this file before you pitch, write a prompt, or review a still or a take.

- **This file wins** where it disagrees with a skill or chapter (any known conflicts are listed at the end). A lane's look block in [LOOKS.md](Skills/nocturnal-dream-image-sets/LOOKS.md) still sets the finish for that lane.
- **REVIEW.md files are history, not rules.** They log rounds, including ideas the owner later rejected. Take rules from this file and the skills.
- **Keep it current.** When the owner gives a new verdict on taste, add or change the rule here, with the date and the test IDs.

## How to work

These habits matter as much as the look. Most failed rounds broke one of them.

1. **Start from the frame the owner liked.** Before prompting for an existing scene or set, name the baseline (the selected take, the locked lane frame, the liked cover), open it, and write down the two or three things in it to keep. Judge every new still side by side against that frame.
2. **Read the owner's words as a feeling, not a shopping list.** Every noun in a prompt gets drawn. "A little Christmas-y" can be one warm window, not wreaths, fairy lights and a coffee cup. "随手拍 / 生活记录" is about how the camera behaves, not about sensor defects and street mess. If a word could mean two different pictures, ask in one line before spending.
3. **Change one thing per round.** After a rejection, adjust what the owner named. Don't swing to the opposite extreme.
4. **Don't grade your own frames.** No "10/10 keeper". Write what you observe, then what the frame keeps or loses against the baseline. The owner decides.
5. **Pitch in writing, test one image, then batch.**

**Worked example: Shanghai After the Snow, 2026-11-26 (v10–v12, 13 stills, 78 credits, all rejected).** The baseline was take 07. You look down at your own footprints, coat edge and boot in frame, then lift up to the buried Bund railing and Pudong. One amber pool of lamplight on clean snow, a navy night, slow snowfall, snow up to the top of the wave railing. The new rounds swung from sunny daylight (v10) to a greeting card with wreaths, fairy lights, a gas lantern and a cup (v11), to a dirty phone-camera street with cardboard, e-bikes and 40–70 cm of snow (v12). v12 was a believable snowy street, so the impossible part was gone. The owner: "it is even worse than the original video". Take 07 was opened only after the third miss.

## The core look

- **Realistic but obviously unreal.** Keep real photo or film texture, but the place must read as impossible at a glance. Test: a viewer asking "where is this?" is a fail; "this can't exist" is a pass. Plausible-big reads as travel photography and fails (T155–T162). Use a designed palette no real place has and an absurd scale. Seedream ignores "as tall as X"; use visible devices instead: cloud crossing the giant, the frame cutting it off, repetition to the horizon. Push the art side (art direction, period film stock, staging) but stop short of illustration (T184–T201).
- **Sit deep in one named niche:** poolcore, 中式梦核, liminal space, megalophobia, weirdcore. Every frame should read as that niche in one second. A beautiful place with a colour change is "just scenery": Red Summer got ~6% likes against 12–14% for the earlier posts (2026-11-26).
- **Alter the space; don't add objects.** Objects added to cluttered rooms (snow in a shop, petals in a banquet hall) were "kinda boring" (T54–T58). The fusion series hit the right aesthetic (T19–T28): a flash-lit familiar interior, a clean seam, and an everyday landscape beyond it.
- **One central subject, few props, big flat colour planes, one strong diagonal.** 永夏 T136 and T101 beat busier frames. A layered "foreground blur + middle + far giant" frame was "messy, no central idea" (T151–T154).
- **An obvious capture style.** The look of the device is the hook: a millennial phone with flash (K800i, N73) or a Canon IXUS 400; iPhone 4 lost (T48–T53). Or use a lane's look block. "Lighting, shader, texture really play a role": blue hour, one warm practical light that is itself the oddity, and film flash, grain and halation (T63–T70).
- **Mood: soothing, comforting, with one oddity.** The Caretaker's feeling: familiar, empty, a little wrong, as if remembered. Never horror, no creatures.
- **Not these:**
  - glowing spectacle such as aurora or the Milky Way filling the frame ("AI slop", T35–T41);
  - serious art or documentary photography methods such as Crewdson, Wall or Sugimoto ("kinda boring", T42–T47);
  - postcard landscapes ("too bland", T01);
  - teen or childhood slice-of-life (T09, T13);
  - couples and plot beats. The exception is the 散场以后 "romantic core, foreign" lane;
  - staged props that turn a frame into a greeting card or an ad.
- **Not China or traditional culture by default** (2026-11-20). When Chinese architecture is the subject, render it faithfully (斗拱, 彩画, 汉白玉, roof ridges) with cinematic awe, and let the oddity lift the building's setting or scale. Never attach a gadget or gag to it (九重门 and 屋顶是山 rejected). A megastructure is one colossal coherent mass in ancient materials, not a stack of small palaces (T218, T220, T226–T230).
- **Four lanes are locked** in [LOOKS.md](Skills/nocturnal-dream-image-sets/LOOKS.md): A on LA02, B on LB04, C on LC07, D on LD04. Take a reference's finish, never its scene.

## Realism and logic

- Before the first still, settle in this order: function and history, story and why, light and weather that serve the mood, planted flaws, lens and film scan (The Beacon Engines).
- No HDR, no perfection, no fake fog, no sky without a mood purpose.
- No smooth AI texture. Don't ask for diffusion filters, lifted blacks or smooth skin. Ask for pores, flyaway hairs, fabric weave and grit, with harder light raking across them. Post grain can't add missing detail. Check every still at a 100% crop (Rebuilding Home, 2026-11-24).
- No pasted-on smiles. Real reactions to events are wanted: a flinch, an eye-roll, a laugh.
- A face that is resting or sleeping outdoors is always in shade, and the prompt says where the shade comes from.
- Check real places against reference photos: landmark spacing, railings, lamps, architecture.

## Prompts

- About 200–300 words is fine. What matters is what is in them:
  - a palette limit ("two main colours only");
  - a one-line Mood;
  - the one impossible change, stated as a plain fact;
  - "the space is empty" when it is.

  List only what should be in the frame. Don't name tripods, rigs or strobes, or Seedream draws them.
- Douyin sets are 9:16, sequels included. Ask before using any other ratio.
- Seedream 5.5 image-to-image edits: a short Chinese prompt ("编辑…只改X，其他一切保持完全不变") keeps the face. A full re-description makes it drift.
- Character face references: the approved sheet plus a real photo only, never a generated frame. Name the identity traits in every prompt.

## Video

- Make video only when motion shows what a still can't: a reveal, a crossing, an event. A "living still" isn't worth the credits.
- Choose the camera for each shot before writing:
  - **fixed:** the life comes from the frame;
  - **handheld:** say where it starts aiming and when that changes;
  - **POV:** decide whether hands appear, and move it like a head.

  Never a generic slow drift.
- One or two actions per shot. Split clips, use no internal timestamps, and write "真实速度，动作不加快". Don't script smiles.
- MVs: open on an event within 1–2 s, show the face open and lit, plan each cut as a hand-off, and vary the perspective.
- Sound: soft, continuous, audible ambience (you hear the wind), no clicks or thunks, one oddity on top of the calm.
- Edit: never tmix or minterpolate Seedance clips (it causes 拖影); use plain `fps=24`. Palette videos use hard beat cuts on a dark background, with no zoom, flashes or transitions. No AI house music.

## Captions

- No questions to the viewer, and no self-posted first comment.
- Short, open, plain lines that add what the photo can't: sound, heat, time, absent people. Never explain the trick, never be cute, and don't keep naming the concept. Match the set's era and register.
