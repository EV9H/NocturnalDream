# Nocturnal Dream AI Studio

Create cinematic **Altered Reality** for the intended Douyin audience. Each scene needs a perceptible cultural anchor, a coherent alteration, and an intended emotion. Draw anchors from contemporary Chinese places and shared experiences as well as history and tradition. Choose an alteration that changes how the space or moment feels. Dreamcore and ancient Chinese cyberpunk, steampunk, or hybrid megastructures belong here when the anchor shapes the idea. Direct even impossible worlds as credible live-action cinema. Everyday traces and people are optional.

Start with **Scene → Sequence → Narrative Film**. Stage 1 is the current focus. Develop the written concept before prompts for a new scene. Consider all six STEPPS lenses and choose two or three that the viewer can experience. Treat audience response as a hypothesis. Plan where the eye lands first, what changes the viewer's understanding, and what the ending gives them time to feel. One shot can do all three. Choose duration by what the material needs.

## Direct the scene

For an open brief, start with the image someone could describe after watching once. Develop candidates by changing the place’s function, history, physical conditions or the viewer’s perception. Keep the audience’s point of recognition specific. For a selected idea, give the director’s written treatment, the spatial or material evidence that makes the alteration believable, and the smallest useful test. Give only the deliverable the user is asking for at this stage.

Choose the world direction for its emotional question. Choose the [photographic territory](references/photographic-territories.md) for the information the camera must reveal. Choose sound from the listener’s position and decide whether music contributes anything the place cannot already convey. Translate the result into a still brief, a motion brief and sound cues when those departments are needed. Mark new recipes as proposals until a reviewed take supports them.

## Read by task

Open the linked chapter for the work you are doing and apply it before drafting or reviewing. Combine routes when a request spans stages. Resolve the links from this skill folder and use current scene files for the brief and take evidence.

For a named or clearly requested creative direction, open the [direction library](references/directions/INDEX.md) and load the matching prompt. Keep directions distinct when comparing them. A newly requested direction takes priority over the tone of previous pitches. Carry a direction into shot design when it belongs to the selected concept. If the user names a photographic look (such as poetic naturalism or sculptural light), use the [six photographic territories](references/photographic-territories.md) directly. A request for daylight, bright color, or an empty space can govern the whole scene.

## Work in the studio

Open the [project workflow](../../../Studio-Reference/PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS_v1.1.md) and copy or consult the [production checklist](../../../Studio-Reference/VIDEO_PRODUCTION_TEMPLATE.md) for active video work. Consult the [Scene First Workbook](../../../Studio-Reference/Scene_First_Workbook_v1.1.xlsx) when planning, logging, or reviewing scenes. Keep the brief, prompt versions, render settings, and reviews in that scene's folder under `Scenes/`. Preserve prior takes and track publication separately.

**Approval gates.** Get approval of the new scene's written creative direction before generating. For any generation that uses credits, tokens, or a limited generation allowance, present the model, output count, and quote or cap, then obtain explicit spending approval. Concept approval and a request to try a scene do not authorize spending. Seek renewed approval when the submission would exceed the approved scope or cap.

After each product, log what to repeat, what failed, and the next controlled test. Use take evidence and timecodes to support any shared checklist improvement. Record adopted changes with version and supporting scene.
