# 04 · Cinematography

How to decide where the camera stands, what the light does, and what the frame holds. Write these decisions down for every shot before any prompt. They become the physical facts in the keyframe and motion prompts.

## The shot brief (fill every line)

1. **Must see / discover late.** "The viewer must see ___. They should discover ___ later."
2. **Camera position.** Place, height, distance to the key subject, what is in front of the lens.
3. **Lens.** Focal length equivalent and what it does here. Focus plane and depth of field.
4. **Composition.** Where the eye lands first, where it goes next, shapes and lines, negative space.
5. **Light plan.** Source, direction, quality, contrast, color temperature, what is lit and what is allowed into shadow.
6. **Color plan.** Dominant, secondary, accent. Carrier object for each and its area.
7. **Materials.** The two or three surfaces that must be believable, and how each answers the light.
8. **Atmosphere.** Haze, rain, snow, steam, dust: what it does for depth, not as decoration.
9. **Motion.** Camera: locked, or one move with start, path, end and reason. World: what moves, how fast, which layers.
10. **Sound position.** Where the listener stands.

## Lens and distance

| Equivalent | Use | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 18–24mm | Immersion, corridors, tight interiors, megastructures from close | Edge distortion, faces stretched. Keep people near center or away. |
| 28–35mm | Default witness view for places and streets | Without foreground it reads flat. Add near occluders. |
| 40–50mm | Natural perspective, human scenes | Fine for most things. |
| 65–85mm | Compression, isolating a detail, stacking lights and layers | Subject separated from place. Keep an environmental cue in frame. |
| 100mm+ | Surveillance feel, flattened skyline, heat shimmer | Needs deliberate reason. |

Height is meaning. Eye height is neutral and witness-like. Low shows weight and threat. High shows system and pattern. Child height changes scale.

## Composition

- Build depth in three planes. **Foreground** occluder or texture (edge of a rail, wet leaf, door frame), **midground** subject, **background** that adds place and scale. Atmosphere separates them.
- Aim for **one dominant shape** readable in silhouette at thumbnail size, plus one counter-shape.
- Place the key subject **off-center** unless order is the idea. Use leading lines to the focal point. Allow negative space that earns its size.
- Vertical 9:16 favors stacked layers, tall structures, reflections, falling or rising motion and doorway framing. A wide landscape view cropped to vertical loses scale: compose natively for 9:16.
- Keep the key subject away from the lower roughly 20% and the right edge, where Douyin's interface sits. (Approximate. Check against the current app.)
- Scale needs a **known-size object in the same plane** as the unknown thing: a person, a bus, a door, a bicycle, a railing.

## Light

- **Motivated.** The viewer believes the source. Name it: window, sodium lamp, shop sign, sun through a gap, headlights.
- **Direction.** Specify relative to the camera: "from camera-right, behind the subject". Side and back light show texture and depth. Frontal light flattens.
- **Quality.** Hard sources make crisp shadows and graphic shapes. Soft sources suit overcast and faces.
- **Contrast.** Choose a ratio. High ratio for drama, low for realism and melancholy. Shadows should hold texture, not crush to flat black unless intended.
- **Color mixing.** Real places mix color temperatures: warm tungsten against cool ambient, green fluorescent against warm window. Use that, with one dominant.
- **Practicals.** Visible lamps and screens are part of the composition. Keep them smaller and dimmer than the main light unless they are the main light.
- **Haze.** Light is only visible in air. Add dust, mist, steam or smoke where you want beams or depth.
- **Highlights.** Control them. Roll off softly, bloom only on real sources.

## Color

- Choose **1 dominant + 1 secondary + 1 optional accent**. Give each a carrier object.
- Neutrals carry the frame. Accent is small and located at the story point.
- Plan a **color event**: where color changes or appears, and what it means.
- Check in grayscale: the subject must separate by value, not only by hue.
- Skin stays natural and readable. Avoid uniform teal-and-orange.

## Materials cheat sheet

Write what the surface does to light and what contact leaves.

- **Snow:** matte with sparkle in raking light, subsurface blue in shadow, wind-packed crust, powder lifting at the edge, compression rim in footprints, melt wetness near warm sources. Depth reads against a known object.
- **Wet stone and asphalt:** darker where wet, reflections only along light-facing planes, dry patches stay matte, puddles show the sky.
- **Metal:** cold highlights, scratches, oxidation in seams, condensation, fingerprints.
- **Glass:** reflections of the opposite room, condensation or rain, edge darkening, double images in old panes.
- **Fabric:** weave and lint in side light, drag when wet, wrinkle lines at joints.
- **Skin:** pores and fine hair in side light, slight shine on forehead and nose, cold-red nose and ears, no uniform gloss.
- **Water:** reflects what is opposite, dark body with highlights, flow lines, wake, foam, small objects riding it.
- **Plastic and linoleum:** soft specular, scuffs along walking paths.
- **Fire, steam, smoke:** light themselves or catch light; give them upward movement and irregular edges.

## Camera motion

- Move only for a reason: to reveal, to follow, to feel a body, to separate layers by parallax. Otherwise lock off.
- State **start, path, end**, speed and the fixed reference that proves the move ("the post exits left while the tower stays fixed").
- Locked-off is a strong choice when the world supplies the motion.
- Handheld means breath and small asymmetries, not shaking. Gimbal glide is smooth and weightless, so use it rarely.
- **POV:** if it is a walker's view, specify head and body: step cadence, a slight dip at contact, gaze changes that lag the body, a visible edge (glove, cuff, shoulder). Add "one continuous shot, no cuts, no zoom".
- Never combine orbit, zoom, crane and pan in one shot.
- Prefer real-time speed. If using slow motion, mark one beat only.

## Time and shot economy

- A short film is a held look plus one change. Choose the duration the change needs.
- Give the establishing read about one second. Give the discovery at least two. Give the end a hold.
- If cutting, each shot must change emotion, add information or raise pressure. Cut shots that do none.
- Long takes are earned by something happening in depth across time.

## Messiness

Clean, centered, evenly lit frames read as AI. Put at least one off-center, imperfect real detail in every frame: a scuffed edge, a repair, clutter, an asymmetric object, an uneven light pool, a trace of use. Not as noise: as something someone left behind.

## Optical realism

Add the imperfections of a real camera, in moderation: slight vignetting, lens-edge softness, micro-contrast, highlight roll-off, shadow noise, subtle chromatic aberration on bright edges, true motion blur. Avoid heavy filters. A film stock or sensor feeling can be described by its effect (fine grain, soft halation on lamps, muted highlights) rather than by naming a camera.
