# 09 · Douyin delivery and the 480p budget

Two fixed constraints shape every scene: the film is watched full-screen on a phone in Douyin, and video generation runs through Higgsfield MCP at **480p to save credits** (the owner's standing decision). Design for both. Do not ask to raise resolution unless the owner does.

Evidence base: a review of Shanghai After the Snow (takes 01–07), One-Minute-to-Harvest, The-Upward-Leak and Class-6-2 stills, 2026-11-19. Observed there: soft 480p detail, no early hook, little happening in 5–10 seconds, prompted details missing from frame 1, evenly clean and symmetric frames, and one scene with no cultural anchor.

## Designing for 480p

480×854 hides small detail and exaggerates mush. So:

- **Big, simple forms.** Make the subject and the evidence of the alteration large in frame. Do not rely on small landmarks, thin railings, fine texture or text. If a detail matters, move closer or enlarge it.
- **Strong value structure.** Separate near, middle and far by brightness and haze, not by fine detail. Check the thumbnail test in grayscale.
- **Fewer, clearer elements.** Three to five things per frame. Repeated fine patterns (snow streaks, crowds, tiles) turn into noise.
- **Close and medium framing** beats distant wide shots. A far skyline at 480p is a few pixels. Put the recognizable landmark large, or let sound and a near object carry the anchor.
- **Grain on purpose.** Add fine grain in the grade. It hides softness and unifies the clip.
- **Spend where it is cheap.** Stills cost far less than video. Put most of the credits into stills at the largest native size: many candidates, choose, retouch. Make few video takes, and make each count.
- **Upscale in post, after selection.** Upscale only the selected take (a dedicated video upscaler or the editor's tool), then re-grain and check faces, edges and flicker. Treat results as unverified until reviewed at phone size. Record the tool and settings.
- **Judge 480p drafts honestly.** Use them for motion, geometry, contact and timing. Judge beauty, texture and light on the still and on the upscaled final, not on the raw draft.
- **Assemble from short takes.** If long single takes fail or cost too much, build a 15–25 second piece from several 4–6 second takes, cut on motion or a shared reference.

## The Douyin viewer

A viewer decides in about one to two seconds, scrolls with a thumb, often with sound on but frequently without headphones. Treat the following as working hypotheses to check against the app and the owner's analytics.

### Hook (first second)
- Open on the most striking, specific frame, not an approach or a fade. The alteration or the anchor must be readable immediately.
- Put the strongest image or the first surprise within the first second. Hold the full reveal for the later payoff.
- The first frame doubles as the cover candidate. Compose it as a vertical poster.

### Pace and payoff
- Something visibly changes every 1.5–2.5 seconds: a move, a reveal, a sound event, a light change.
- Place the payoff before the halfway point if the clip is short (under 10 seconds), or at roughly two-thirds for longer ones, then hold for the feeling.
- Make the payoff one clear, physical event, not a subtle drift. If a viewer has to wait to see anything, they leave.
- Prefer a clip that **loops** well: the last image rhymes with the first. Test, do not assume.

### Frame
- Compose natively for 9:16. Keep key subjects away from the bottom roughly 20% (caption and account area) and the right edge (buttons), and away from the top roughly 10%. Approximate: verify in the app.
- Do not rely on text. If a place name is needed, use a short, clean title placed in the safe zone, or put it in the post description. No AI-generated signage.
- Strong vertical layering works: foreground edge, midground subject, tall background.

### Sound
- Assume it may be heard on a phone speaker. Make the hero sound simple and mid-frequency. Do not hide the idea in sub-bass or in quiet detail.
- Local audio memory is powerful (广播, 报站, 小区喇叭, 叫卖, 麻将). Use the owner's real recordings or clean recreations where possible.
- Consider on-screen text off by default. Provide the description and hashtags separately.

### Post copy (separate deliverable)
- Draft a title (short, curiosity or recognition, in Chinese), a one-line description and three to five hashtags per release. Keep them factual and non-clickbait. Treat copy performance as a test.

## Pre-approval checks (fidelity before spending more)

Before approving any further video spend on a scene, confirm on the **first frame** and on the **keyframe**:

1. Every named object in the prompt is present and correct (colors, shoes, gloves, lamp count, light direction).
2. The light matches the plan, and no large unplanned color wash.
3. The anchor is recognizable at 480p without a caption.
4. The payoff event is large enough to see at thumbnail size.
5. At least one off-center imperfection (dirt, clutter, an asymmetry, a mistake of use) is present.
6. The first second has a hook.

If one fails, fix the still or the prompt before generating.

## Slate checks

- Every scene needs a **real cultural anchor** the audience recognizes without explanation. A scene with none (a generic industrial room, an anonymous interior) is not finished.
- Rotate looks and mechanisms. The existing slate leans on weather and single-droplet mechanisms, both flagged as thin.
- Prefer concepts where the payoff can be one big physical event visible at 480p.
