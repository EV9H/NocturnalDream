# 02 · Concept

Turn a brief into an idea worth filming. Output for this stage is a short pitch (core image first), then a one-page treatment for the chosen one. No prompts yet.

## The idea equation

**Cultural Anchor × Altered Reality × Emotional Recognition**

### Cultural Anchor

Something the intended Douyin audience recognizes from sound or picture within two seconds. Draw on contemporary life as much as tradition.

- **Everyday China:** 老小区楼道和声控灯, 菜市场, 夜市, 小卖部, 筒子楼, 公交报站, 地铁换乘通道, 电梯里的广告屏, 校园广播和课间操, 澡堂, 理发店, 麻将馆, 绿皮火车, 春运, 高架桥下的球场, 小区门口的快递柜, 城中村握手楼.
- **Landmarks and geography:** 外滩与陆家嘴, 重庆的立交和轻轨穿楼, 广州的骑楼, 苏州河, 西湖, 长江大桥, 青藏线, 黄土高原窑洞, 福建土楼, 江南水乡.
- **Shared rituals and memory:** 春节, 中秋, 高考季, 暑假的午后, 停电的夜晚, 梅雨回南天, 第一场雪.
- **History and craft:** 城门, 治水, 浑天仪, 榫卯, 茶, 戏台, 年画.

Write down why this audience knows it and which sound, object or layout proves it. If a recognizable sign must carry the anchor, the anchor is weak.

### Altered Reality

The alteration has to be a **system**, not an effect. Write all four lines:

1. **Rule.** The one thing that changed (what, how much).
2. **Mechanism.** Why it behaves the way it does. Physical, infrastructural, historical or perceptual.
3. **Evidence.** What in the frame a viewer can read as proof (height marks, repairs, worn paths, tied ropes, relocated signs, tools).
4. **Consequence.** How life, objects or space adapted. This is what makes it believable and moving.

Weather, a filter, a color cast, "more fog" or "more neon" are conditions, not systems. They can be part of a system, never the whole alteration.

Alteration families to rotate through when brainstorming:

| Family | Question | Example seed (unproven) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Function | What if this place did a different job? | A rooftop water tank farm in 老小区 becomes the neighborhood's clock, tuned by tapping. |
| Scale | What if it were far bigger, smaller or heavier? | A single 过街天桥 runs for ten kilometers as someone's commute. |
| Time | What if different eras coexist physically? | A 弄堂 where each doorstep lies in a different decade of light, from gas lamps to LED. |
| History fork | What if one technology or event had gone another way? | Imperial water clocks scaled into a city-wide engine that sets the market bell. |
| Perception | What if the way we sense it changed? | A subway platform where announcements arrive a minute before the train, and people stand to listen. |
| Material | What if a substance behaved differently? | Dew on a 回南天 wall condenses into a slow, readable map of every previous resident's handprints. |
| Absence/presence | What if something vanished or stayed? | A school's 课间操 music keeps playing in an empty campus at dusk, and the afterimages of lines stay on the field. |

Seeds are prompts to think with, not approved concepts.

### Emotional Recognition

Name the feeling in one word and the moment that causes it, as a physical event the viewer sees or hears. Nostalgia, awe, loneliness, tenderness, vertigo, quiet. The camera lets the moment last. The film never states the feeling in text or voice-over.

## Brainstorming procedure

1. Restate the brief as: anchor wanted, mood wanted, constraints (day or night, people or none, duration).
2. Generate **three candidates from three different alteration families**. They must differ in what the viewer experiences, not in props.
3. For each give: core image (one sentence), anchor and why, alteration (rule, mechanism, evidence, consequence), the feeling and its moment, the hardest thing to film.
4. Score each 0–2 on: Image test, Swap test, Ambition ladder level, Filmability today, Originality against the current slate.
5. Recommend one and say why. State what the others would be better for.
6. For "give me another" requests, read the existing scenes first and change the core relationship, not the props. Respect any user rejection and its reason.

## Treatment (one page, after a choice)

Write it as a director would, in prose plus a few labeled lines:

- **Logline.** One sentence.
- **First frame.** Where the camera stands, height, what is in front, what is lit.
- **The turn.** What the viewer understands second, and from what evidence.
- **The held moment.** The final beat and how long we stay.
- **World rules and evidence.** Short list of what is visible that proves them.
- **Look.** One named look from [03](03-worlds-and-looks.md) and why it fits the emotion.
- **Sound.** Listener position, three layers, where silence falls.
- **STEPPS.** Two or three viewer-facing levers only (Emotion, Social Currency, Triggers, Public, Practical Value, Stories). Everything is a hypothesis until audience data exists.
- **Hardest problem and the smallest test.** What will most likely fail and how a still or short take will check it.

## Lessons carried over from past scenes

- Shanghai After the Snow worked best when snow depth was readable against a familiar fixture, and when the camera turn revealed the same storm across the river. It stayed shallow because the alteration was weather only. Deepen with a rule and consequence (for example why the snow stayed, how the city adapted).
- Small mechanical gimmicks (a shadow wandering, patterns flying off a mahjong table) give the viewer one thing to watch and little to feel. Prefer changes that rewrite a whole space or a shared routine.
- Real locations fail on small facts. Check landmark spacing, railing design and street furniture against photos before spending.
