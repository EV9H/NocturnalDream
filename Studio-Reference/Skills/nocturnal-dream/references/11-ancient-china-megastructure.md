# 11 · Ancient-China steampunk megastructure

An epic world direction: real ancient Chinese engineering, pushed down an alternate technology history until it holds up a city, a mountain or a river. Steampunk, cyberpunk or a hybrid are all allowed, but the culture must come first. Combine with the Megastructure Sublime look in [03](03-worlds-and-looks.md), the Douyin rules in [09](09-douyin-and-480p.md) and the audience chapter [10](10-audience-and-popularity.md).

Facts below are from general knowledge and must be **checked against sources before a scene uses them**. Mark invented parts as alternate history.

## The method: real system, forked history, daily life

1. **Pick a real system or artifact** (list below). Learn how it actually worked.
2. **Fork it.** Ask: what if it had kept scaling, found a new power source, or become the city's organizing machine?
3. **Scale it** to building, mountain or river size.
4. **Derive the consequences:** who tends it, how the city keeps time, how trade, weather, festival and noise change, what breaks, what is patched.
5. **Show it at the level of an ordinary morning**, not a war or a chosen one. The wonder comes from seeing people live under it.

A fork needs a rule and a cost, like any alteration in [02](02-concept.md).

Always state the **era anchor** (for example Han, Tang, Song or Ming) and keep clothing, architecture and tools consistent with it. A hybrid with later or fantastical tech should be declared once, in the treatment.

## Visual grammar

- **Materials:** bronze, iron, lacquered and raw timber, rope and bamboo, grey brick, glazed and plain tile, silk and paper, stone. Show patina, tool marks, rope wear, soot, repair.
- **Structure:** visible load paths. Brackets, joints, counterweights, guide ropes and braces explain why the giant thing stands. Gears are wooden and bronze, with plausible teeth and lubricant stains.
- **Steam and smoke need a plausible source:** kitchens, bathhouses, foundries, tea stalls, salt boiling, hot springs. Show the vent, the fuel store and the soot on the roof.
- **Life at human scale:** carriers, boatmen, tea vendors, children, laundry, chickens. They keep the world from being empty spectacle.
- **Palette:** a dominant earth or bronze family with one accent (vermilion, lamp amber, jade). Avoid a rainbow of effects.
- **Light:** one dominant source cut by structure (sun through lattices, furnace glow, lantern grids), with atmospheric haze for depth.

## Cliches to avoid

- Neon dragons and "Chinese cyberpunk" pasted on modern skylines.
- Gears glued onto pagodas for decoration. If a gear exists, it does a visible job.
- Glowing blue circuits on hanfu or temple eaves.
- A generic red-lantern alley treated as the whole identity.
- Mixing dynasties at random. Pick one anchor and keep it.
- Landmarks with the wrong style (check roof forms, bracket sets, bridge types).

## Scale craft

- **Reveal shot:** begin on a small human activity in the foreground. Move or wait so the giant system is revealed behind, with atmosphere separating the planes.
- **A known-size object in the same plane:** a person on a roof ridge, a boat on the canal, a porter on the stair.
- **Interaction:** the megastructure acts on the ordinary world. A cup trembles, a lamp sways, a bell is rung by the movement.
- **Inside the machine:** one tactile close shot (a bronze tooth meeting a wooden one, a rope under strain) sells that the machine is real.
- **Time:** let it move slowly and with weight. Big things start late and stop slowly.

## Designing for 480p

Megastructures lose small detail first, so:
- Use a few giant silhouettes (a ring, a tower, a bridge, a wheel) with fog layers between them.
- Keep windows, ornament and rope detail large or leave them out.
- Rely on steam plumes, bells and creaks to convey scale. Do not rely on sub-bass: phone speakers lose it. Use mid-frequency sounds (a bronze bell, timber groan, rope tension, drip, gong).
- Put the payoff in one big motion, such as a ring turning, a gate opening or a wheel lifting water.

## Sound

Water drip and clepsydra tick, wooden gear clatter, bronze bell, drum, gong, rope creak, canal water, steam hiss, vendors' cries, bamboo pipe whistle. Choose a listener position (on the porter's stair, inside the machine hall, on the canal) and keep it.

## Douyin angle (hypotheses)

- **Levers:** Emotion (awe), Social Currency ("原来古代可以这样想象", pride and discovery), Triggers (a recurring world), Public (a recognizable signature structure). A shared world suits a series.
- **Hook:** the scale reveal in the first second, with a human cue visible.
- **Comment bait:** the real artifact behind the fork. Viewers like to correct and add ("这是自贡的井架吧"). Credit it in the description to turn fact-checkers into commenters.
- **Accuracy matters.** The audience knows ancient China. Label the alternate history, and keep it precise where it touches the real.
- Check Douyin's rules for AI-generated content labels.

## Pitch check

For each pitch, answer: which real system, which era, what the fork is, what the rule and cost are, what ordinary morning we see, what is the first-second image, and what the first three comments will be.

## Starting seeds (proposals only, none tested)

1. **The Brine Forest (自贡).** Bamboo derricks as tall as pagodas rise out of morning mist. Natural-gas flames boil brine in iron pans along terraces. A porter climbs a bamboo scaffold with a basket as the first steam rolls over the roofs.
2. **The Ring That Sets the Market.** A bronze ring the size of a hill turns once an hour above a tile-roofed district. Teacups tremble, the 报时 drum answers, and the first stalls open.
3. **The Lock-Stair City (灵渠 / 大运河).** A canal climbs a hillside as a staircase of locks driven by water wheels. A boat rises past kitchens, bathhouses and sleeping cats while the lock gate groans open.
