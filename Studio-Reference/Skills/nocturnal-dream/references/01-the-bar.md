# 01 · The bar

What separates festival-level AI film from the usual output, and how to keep this studio on the right side of it.

## What "master-class realism, highly artful" means here

Realism is the surface. Art is the point of view. Both are required.

- **Specific.** A named place, a particular hour, a certain material, an exact distance. "A snowy street at night" is generic. "The granite lamp plinth on the west Bund, snow level with the second rail, one amber lamp behind your right shoulder" is a place.
- **Pressured.** Something is at stake in the frame: scale against a human, a system pushing on a place, a thing that will happen in two seconds, a light about to change. A calm picture with nothing pushing on it is wallpaper.
- **Seen from somewhere.** The camera has a position, a height, a reason, and sometimes a limit. It is a witness, not a drone.
- **Authored.** Composition, color and light carry meaning. Someone chose what is lit, what is lost in dark, and where the eye lands first.
- **Imperfect like reality.** Real footage has grain, falloff, flare discipline, uneven light, dirt, wear, off-center subjects. Perfection is the first AI tell.
- **Held.** Time is a tool. The shot stays long enough for the viewer to understand and feel, then stops before it explains.
- **Heard.** Sound is half the realism. A silent-looking film with generic ambience reads as fake.

## Why past output was boring

Diagnosed from Shanghai After the Snow. Each is a rule below.

1. The alteration was only weather: a filter on a real place, not a changed system.
2. Everything defaulted to one palette (charcoal, midnight blue, one amber lamp) and one safe eye-level wide view.
3. Prompts told the model what to avoid more than what to build, so it produced the average of the category.
4. Too many actions and details crammed into one short take, none given room.
5. One generation per decision, so there was no selection pressure.
6. 480p drafts were composed like high-resolution work (distant landmarks, fine snow, thin rails), so detail turned to mush. 480p is a fixed credit decision, so design for it ([09](09-douyin-and-480p.md)).
7. No hook in the first second and little visible change in 5–10 seconds. Payoffs were subtle or arrived late.
8. Prompted details (boot color, glove, lamp) were lost in the first frame and nobody checked.
9. Frames were tidy and symmetric, with no mess, wear or odd detail. One scene had no cultural anchor at all.

## The six tests

Run these on every concept, still and take. A fail means revise before spending more.

1. **One-image test.** After one viewing, can someone describe the single image they remember in one sentence, with no mention of the tool or style?
2. **Swap test.** Replace the anchor with another city or object. If the frames and feeling barely change, the anchor is decoration. Rework the concept.
3. **Thumbnail test.** Shrink to phone thumbnail width and turn to grayscale. Is there one clear focal point, readable depth (near, middle, far) and a silhouette?
4. **Mute test.** With sound off, does the idea still read? Then with sound on, does sound add something the picture cannot?
5. **Physics test.** Pause on contacts: foot in snow, hand on metal, water on stone, shadow under an object. Do weight, shadow, reflection and wear agree? Do small things keep their identity from first frame to last?
6. **Friend test.** Would a viewer text this to a friend with one sentence? If the sentence is only "cool AI video", it fails.

## Be bold: the idea checklist

A strong idea usually has at least three of these.

- **A system, not an effect:** a rule that explains why this is happening and what else it causes.
- **A scale event:** something much bigger, slower or older than a person, with a human-size cue in the same frame.
- **A reveal by perception:** the audience understands the change a beat after seeing it, from evidence, not a caption.
- **A cost:** someone or something has adapted, repaired, patched or paid for the alteration. Adaptation makes it believable.
- **A sensory surprise:** one sound or light behavior the viewer has never experienced here.
- **A memory trigger:** a sound, object or routine Chinese viewers recognize without explanation.

## Ban list

Do not write these words in prompts or treatments: cinematic, epic, stunning, breathtaking, masterpiece, 8K, hyperrealistic, award-winning, beautiful lighting, dynamic camera, ultra detailed, trending. They give the model nothing to film. Replace each with the fact you meant: lens, light direction, material, action.

Do not write feelings as instructions ("sad", "mysterious", "magical"). Give the cause the viewer can see or hear.

## AI tells to design against

| Tell | What to do instead |
| --- | --- |
| Waxy skin, plastic surfaces | Name texture and its light response: pores in side light, wool lint, scuffed enamel, wet concrete with dry patches. |
| Uniform streaks, repeated patterns (snow, rain, crowds) | Layer depth, vary speed and size, add occlusion by foreground objects, make some areas empty. |
| Everything evenly lit | Use one dominant source, let shadow areas stay dark with texture, light only what the eye needs. |
| Dead-center symmetrical subject | Off-center the subject, let leading lines and negative space do the work. Use symmetry only when the idea is order. |
| Teal and orange by default, bloom everywhere | Pick a palette from the world. Control highlights. Halation only on real sources. |
| Constant slow push-in or floating drone | One motivated move with a reason, or locked off. |
| Everything in slow motion | Real time speed. Use slow motion once, for one beat, with a reason. |
| Over-clean sets | Add wear, repair, dirt, clutter, signs of use, with specifics. |
| Backgrounds that melt | Fewer, larger, clearer background forms. Real landmarks held in a reference image. |
| Morphing hands, extra fingers | Keep hands occluded, out of frame or doing a simple, single contact. |
| Gibberish signage | Avoid legible text, or supply it in a reference and keep it large and simple. Chinese characters: keep to short, common words and verify. |

## Ambition ladder

Before approving a concept, name where it sits:

1. **Pretty.** A place looks nice. Reject.
2. **Altered.** A place has a visible alteration. Insufficient on its own.
3. **Altered with consequences.** The alteration has a rule, evidence and adaptation visible in the frame.
4. **Felt.** The consequences are experienced through a held moment of sound, light or scale, and the viewer completes the thought.

Ship at level 4 only.
