# Example prompts

Covers the owner liked. Read them for tone, length and viewpoint, not to copy. Each was used on Seedream 5.5 at 3:4.

## The Same Afternoon

```text
Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: a found family snapshot from the late 1980s, shot on a Pentax K1000 with a 50mm f/1.7 lens on Kodak Ektachrome 100 slide film that has aged for decades: colours shifted warm and slightly magenta, highlights creamy and blooming, blacks lifted to brown, fine dust specks and one faint hairline scratch on the scan, soft vignette.

Camera: handheld at standing adult eye height in the doorway of a living room, angled slightly down and a little crooked, focus on the middle of the room so the doorframe edge at the left is a soft dark blur.

Scene: an ordinary lived-in suburban living room at about 5 pm. Low golden sun comes through a single window with half-drawn sheer curtains and lays one long, sharp rectangle of light across a patterned carpet and up the side of a fabric armchair. A small side table holds a ceramic cup of tea with a thin thread of steam rising through the sunbeam, a folded newspaper, reading glasses. A wooden cabinet with a few framed family photos, a houseplant, a clock on the wall, a television that is off. No people. Dust floats visibly in the sunbeam.

Mood: perfectly calm and familiar, but the light is a little too golden and too still, like a memory that has been replayed many times. Quiet, tender, slightly uncanny.

Avoid: modern furniture, screens, HDR, sharp digital clarity, text.
```

## The Ballroom

```text
Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7 with a 43mm wide lens, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, long exposure on a tripod (about 4 seconds), printed warm. Heavy grain in the shadows, warm sodium-gold tones, deep crushed blacks, highlights on the chandelier softly blooming with halation.

Camera: tripod at waist height at the far end of a grand old hotel ballroom, level and centred, looking down the length of the empty parquet floor toward a small stage with closed velvet curtains.

Scene: 3 am. The ballroom is empty and slightly decayed: a vast sprung wooden floor with worn patches, tall gilded mirrors with silver spotting, round tables at the sides with white cloths and chairs pushed back, a few fallen paper streamers, one chandelier lit at half brightness. In the exact middle of the floor, one couple is still slow-dancing. Because of the long exposure they are a translucent, motion-blurred ghost: a dark suit and a pale dress smeared into a soft swirl, their feet the only slightly sharper part. Dust hangs in the air in the chandelier's light. Their reflection is faint and blurred in the polished floor.

Mood: the party ended long ago and nobody told them. Haunted but tender, not horror. Still, quiet, the feeling of half-remembered music.

Avoid: horror cues, faces, readable text, clean modern interiors.
```

## The Pool at 4am

```text
Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: early-2000s digital compact camera snapshot (Canon PowerShot, 2003), small sensor, slightly noisy shadows, a cool fluorescent white balance, mild chromatic fringing, a little lens distortion at the edges, slightly soft corners. Not cinematic, not polished.

Camera: handheld at sitting height from one of the gate seats, wide angle (about 28mm equivalent), looking across the departure gate toward the dark windows, framing a little tilted.

Scene: an airport departure gate at 4 am. Where the carpet should be, the whole gate area is a perfectly still, clear indoor swimming pool, about knee deep, with pale blue tiles and lane-line markings on the bottom. Rows of connected metal airport seats stand along its edges and two rows stand directly in the shallow water. Underwater pool lights glow turquoise, throwing moving caustic light patterns across the low ceiling and the seat backs. Above, the departure screens glow but are blurred and unreadable. Through the tall windows, darkness and the blurred lights of a parked plane. One lone traveller sits on the pool edge in a hoodie with a backpack beside them, jeans rolled up, feet in the water, seen from behind and small in the frame. Everything else is empty.

Mood: liminal, silent, the hour when nothing is supposed to be happening. Calm, lonely, dreamlike, completely believable.

Avoid: people's faces, readable text, logos, cinematic grading.
```

## The Last Bus

```text
Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: contemporary 35mm film, Leica M6 with a 35mm f/2 lens wide open, CineStill 800T tungsten film shot at night: strong red halation around every bright light, cool teal shadows, visible grain, shallow focus.

Camera: handheld from a window seat near the back of an almost empty night bus, at a sitting passenger's eye height, angled slightly forward through the window glass. Focus is on the scene outside; raindrops on the glass in the foreground are soft circles of light. The bus interior is reflected faintly over the view: a row of empty seats, a yellow handrail, the fluorescent ceiling strip.

Scene: the bus has stopped at a bus stop that should not exist: a lone, ordinary glass bus shelter with a lit advertising panel (blank, glowing white) standing in the middle of a dark open field of tall grass at night, with no road and no buildings anywhere, only low fog lying over the grass and a faint line of distant hills. The shelter's light makes a pale pool on the grass. Nobody is waiting. The bus doors are open; their light spills out onto the wet grass at the bottom of the frame.

Mood: the half-asleep feeling of missing your stop on the last bus home. Quiet, lonely, strange but calm, like a dream you will forget by morning.

Avoid: readable text, logos, people's faces, horror cues.
```

## The Last Reel

```text
Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1996 photograph on a Contax G2 with a 35mm f/2 lens, handheld at 1/8 second on Fujicolor Superia 800 pushed one stop: deep green-black shadows, saturated colour, coarse grain, slight motion smear on anything moving, soft halation around the brightest highlights, a few scratches and dust on the scan. Reference: Christopher Doyle's cinematography for Wong Kar-wai, with Hiroshi Sugimoto's Theaters.

Camera: low in the aisle of an old single-screen cinema, about seat height, three rows from the back, looking toward the screen. The red velvet seat-back nearest the lens is out of focus and fills the lower left corner. Framing slightly crooked.

Scene: after closing, around midnight. Rows of worn red velvet seats, all empty, an exit-sign box without readable text, peeling plaster columns. One thick projector beam cuts across the upper half of the frame from the booth behind, full of hanging dust. On the large screen plays an old home movie: a faded 1980s beach, a child running into small waves, the image soft and flickering. In one seat in the middle of the empty house, a man in a projectionist's cardigan sits alone, seen from behind, his head and shoulders a soft silhouette, slightly blurred.

Light: one source. The colour of the whole room comes from the screen: the blue-turquoise sea of the home movie washes the seat-backs, the man's shoulders and the dust in the beam with cold blue-green light. The projector beam itself is bright with a faint warm core. Everything off-axis falls to green-black, about 70 percent of the frame in darkness.

Mood: someone is watching a memory that may not be his. Quiet, longing, slightly unreal, as if the film is the only warm thing left in the building.

Avoid: readable text, logos, faces, horror cues, modern multiplex interior, clean digital sharpness, HDR, cinematic teal-orange grading.
```
