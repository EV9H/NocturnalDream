# The Street That Docks / 会靠岸的街

**SC-008 (provisional) · Brief v0.1 · Stage 1 scene · Production: rejected at concept review · Publication: unscheduled**

The user rejected the pitch because its fictional infrastructure had too little connection to a specific cultural memory or lived experience. The draft is retained as a record of that creative decision. Generation spend was zero.

Working copy of the [video production checklist v1.1](../../Studio-Reference/VIDEO_PRODUCTION_TEMPLATE.md). Proposed format is one vertical shot, roughly 12–15 seconds to accommodate the separation and a final hold. Generation budget and settings are pending.

## 1. Scene brief

In a fictional estuary town, a short market street built on a steel pontoon serves both banks. Before dawn it leaves the residential quay and crosses to the fishing quay for the morning catch. A fishmonger can wheel loaded crates onto the same street that customers walk onto later from the other bank.

The market shares a paving pattern with its two landings, so it reads as a continuation of the town when moored. Its row of low tiled stalls, a shuttered fish counter, a bowl at the open noodle stall and a bicycle waiting on the fixed quay establish scale. A worn steel bridge plate spans the near seam. Dock fenders and a low floating hull become legible as the market pulls away. The pontoon travels as one rigid assembly, deck level and waterline consistent. The bridge plate is retracted and the crossing gate closed before the pontoon moves.

**Discovery.** From the fixed quay, see the bike, matched paving and the lit noodle counter. Under the bike's sightline, the steel plate draws back. A dark seam opens and widens into moving water. Hold as the row of shops travels intact, its reflected light stretching into the wake. Across the water, a second landing with the same sill hints at where it goes.

**STEPPS hypotheses.** Emotion comes from the moment a familiar, warm street acquires the scale and movement of a vessel. Social Currency comes from the image someone can describe to a friend as a market street that crosses the river to meet the fishing boats. Triggers, Public, Practical Value and Stories were considered for the scene. Audience response is unmeasured.

**Reference needs.** Ferry landing for plate and fender geometry, fish-market lane for paving and signs of use, and a vessel leaving a sheltered quay for water displacement and sound distance. Source and rights to be logged when references are selected. Setting is fictional.

## 2. Art direction and shot plan

**Midnight Witness with Editorial Rupture.** A fixed, eye-height, 28 mm equivalent view from just behind the quay gate, oblique to the joining seam so the street and water stay legible together in 9:16. Keep the bicycle and stone curb in the near lower corner, the shop row in the middle, and the second landing in the distance. Hold focus deep enough to resolve the seam and the noodle counter. The pontoon is the dominant visual event.

Soft amber light from the counter travels with the pontoon, grazing its paving and breaking into bands on the disturbed water. A fixed quay lamp behind camera-left casts a firmer cross-light over the bicycle and stone edge. Cool midnight-blue ambient fill keeps fenders and masonry readable in charcoal shadow. Thin estuary haze separates the far landing, with the hull edge and water gap crisp. Preserve texture in practical highlights and keep the stationary quay and moving market's shadows tied to their light sources.

**Sound.** Begin with the close plate scrape and the low room sound of an open stall. A restrained drive hum enters as the deck moves. Water laps louder against the newly exposed fixed quay as bowl and counter sounds recede across the gap. No dialogue or score planned for the first picture test.

| Anchor or object | Fixed features and location | Allowed movement or state change | Where to verify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Near quay | Stone curb, bicycle, closed gate, fenders | Bridge plate retracts onto fixed quay before departure | Start, separation, hold |
| Market deck | One low hull, one continuous paving grid, tiled posts and noodle counter in fixed relative positions | Whole deck translates away as one rigid body | Start, mid-gap, hold |
| Water and far landing | Far sill fixed, hull waterline consistent | Water enters the opening gap and a modest wake breaks moving reflections | Before, during and after separation |

**First test.** After approval, evaluate a docked keyframe and a matching separated layout. With the point of view and practical lights held fixed, can both states read at phone size as the same street, with a clear seam, a believable hull and an unmistakably stationary quay? Motion can then test one clean separation and hold. Check that the water response starts where the hull displaces it. Plate motion can stay outside the motion test if it compromises the primary comparison.
