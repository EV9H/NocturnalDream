One continuous 3:4 shot, 4 seconds, locked-off camera, no camera movement, no cuts, no zoom, no on-screen text, no subtitles.
Image 1 is the first frame and sets the place, the light, the tilted framing and the old phone-camera look with flash, noise and JPEG grain; keep that look for the whole shot.
The camera stays perfectly still at the tilted angle of Image 1. The mood is calm and comforting, a warm summer night, with one strange thing happening on certain screens.
0–4s, wind: From the very first frame, a strong, steady night wind blows from the left across the field beyond the ceiling edge. The long grass bends far over and rolls in big waves that travel visibly from left to right across the whole field, the blades flashing pale where the monitors' blue light catches them. The grass keeps moving strongly for the whole shot, in slow gusts that swell and ease.
0–4s, screens: Most monitors glow steadily. Three of them flicker irregularly, as if a weak signal were reaching them: the third monitor from the left in the row out in the grass, the large monitor in the grass on the right nearest the room, and, inside the room, the monitor behind the green tea bottle. Their screens stutter between the blue desktop and a soft bright white, two or three quick flickers, a pause, then again, never in a regular rhythm. Sometimes the monitor in the room flickers at the same moment as one out in the grass, as if answering it.
Fixed: the camera does not move; every flicker happens only inside the glass of the screens, and the plastic monitor casings stay unchanged; the other monitors keep a steady glow; the ceiling tiles and the clean edge where the ceiling stops over the field stay in the same place; desks, chairs, keyboards, the green tea bottle and noodle cups keep their shape and position; the fluorescent tube at upper right stays on; the pylon and the small light on the horizon stay fixed against the deep blue night sky; flash exposure and grain stay steady.
Sound: listening from the empty chair beside the nearest desk. The main sound is the wind: a full, soft rushing of wind through long grass, clearly heard, rising and falling in slow swells with the waves in the field, warm and soothing. Under it, a faint low hum of old computers and distant crickets. When the screens flicker, a soft, faint electric buzz swells and fades with them. Everything stays smooth and gentle, with no clicks, no beeps and no thuds. No music, no speech.
