A single real photograph, one natural exposure, 16:9 landscape, from an adult's eye height on the brick walkway of the Ming Great Wall at Jinshanling, northeast of Beijing, about twenty minutes before sunrise in late autumn. 24mm lens, level horizon, handheld.

Light. The sun is still below the far ridges on the right. The sky runs from deep blue overhead and on the left to a pale, slightly warm band low on the right horizon. That band is the only real source: everything facing right picks up a thin pale edge, and everything facing the camera is in soft shadow. The camera is exposed for the sky, so the near walkway and the parapet sit dark and dim, with heavy, noisy shadows and little colour. The upward-facing step treads catch a faint cool sheen from the sky and are lighter than the dark risers. Nothing in the foreground is evenly lit.

Distance. Each plane is paler, bluer and lower in contrast than the one in front of it. The near bricks are dark and textured; the watchtower is greyer and softer; the engine a kilometre away is a blue-grey mass with little detail except where the horizon light edges its right side; the farthest ridges and engines are flat pale shapes with no detail at all. Mist lies in the valleys and glows faintly where it faces the horizon.

The scene. Worn grey brick steps climb straight away from the camera between a crenellated outer parapet on the left and a low inner wall on the right. A porter in a faded padded jacket climbs fifteen metres ahead, back to the camera, a shoulder pole with two bundles of firewood. The steps lead up to a two-storey watchtower on the crest. Directly behind and far above that watchtower, on the next summit, stands the beacon engine, ten times the watchtower's height: a tapering grey brick base, then storeys of dark timber with deep bracket sets under grey tiled eaves, large bronze gear wheels between the storeys, a wooden water wheel in the base fed by a long timber flume on trestles, its spill a thin white line down the slope. Its top is an open crown of separate upright bronze vanes around an unlit iron fire bowl, its tips in a thin band of cloud, standing out as a dark silhouette against the brighter sky. People on its galleries are specks. Two small oil lanterns glow on its lowest gallery, warm and unequal. To the right, the Wall runs on over lower ridges, each crowned with a smaller engine, fading into the haze.

Wear and imperfection. Lichen and moss in the mortar joints, step centres hollowed by feet, a few replaced bricks lighter in tone, stumps and cut timber on the logged slope below the engine.
