A single real photograph, one natural exposure, 16:9 landscape, from an adult's eye height on the brick walkway of the Ming Great Wall at Jinshanling, northeast of Beijing, about twenty minutes before sunrise in late autumn. 24mm lens, slightly tilted a degree off level, handheld.

Light. The sun is below the far ridges on the right. The camera is exposed for the sky, so the near walkway and parapet are dark and dim, with heavy, noisy shadows and little colour. Only the upward-facing step treads catch a faint cool sheen. Each plane further away is paler, bluer and lower in contrast than the one in front of it.

Sky. A broken field of low stratocumulus covers two thirds of the sky, uneven in size and thickness, with a ragged torn gap above the engine. On the right, the cloud bases glow dull orange and rose from below; on the left they are heavy blue-grey with darker undersides. Thin streaks of high cirrus above the gap. A grey smoke trail from the engine's crown leans and shears to the left in the wind.

Scale. The steps climb to a two-storey watchtower on the near crest. Behind the watchtower the mountain drops into a deep valley filled with mist, and beyond that valley, on a much higher mountain, stands the beacon engine. Its base is hidden in the valley mist. It rises from the mist far above the watchtower: the watchtower reaches no higher than the engine's lowest eave in the frame, and the engine's crown pushes into the cloud base. Because it is far away, the engine is a pale blue-grey shape, much lighter and softer than the dark watchtower in front of it, with detail only where the horizon light edges its right side. People on its galleries are invisible dots.

The engine. A tapering grey brick base, then storeys of dark timber with deep bracket sets under grey tiled eaves, large bronze gear wheels between the storeys, a long timber flume on trestles climbing the mountain to a water wheel in its base, its spill a thin white thread down the slope. The top is an open crown of upright bronze vanes around an iron fire bowl: one vane is bent outward and one is missing, so the crown is uneven. One storey is patched with newer pale planks, and a bamboo scaffold clings to its left side. Of three lanterns on its lowest gallery, one burns bright, one is dim, one is dark.

Planted flaws in the near Wall. On the left parapet, the third and fourth merlons have lost their tops, and their bricks lie as a small rubble heap on the walkway edge. A wild jujube sapling grows out of a crack in the parapet. Dry grass grows in the joints of the steps. The step heights are uneven, and one step near the bottom has a broken corner. Bricks vary in tone from blue-grey to brown-grey, with a few rust-red ones, some with spalled faces and washed-out mortar. The low inner wall on the right leans slightly and bulges. Frost whitens the shaded top edges of the parapet but not the rest. Dropped twigs of firewood and a frayed rope lie on the steps.

Porter. Fifteen metres up the steps, a porter in a faded, patched padded jacket climbs away from the camera, leaning forward, the shoulder pole bending, one bundle of firewood larger than the other.

The watchtower. One upper corner has crumbled, one arched window is bricked up, and grass grows along its top.

Distance. To the right, the Wall runs on over lower ridges toward the horizon. Each ridge carries a smaller engine: the nearest is half hidden by a cloud shadow, one is unfinished inside bamboo scaffolding, and the farthest are flat pale shapes. Mist lies unevenly in the valleys, thick in some and absent in others.
