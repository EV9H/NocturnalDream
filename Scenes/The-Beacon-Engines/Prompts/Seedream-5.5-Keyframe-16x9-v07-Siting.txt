Layout, as a real place. The camera faces along the Wall. On the left is the Wall's outer side, facing the steppe: a crenellated parapet with merlons and loopholes. On the right is the inner side: a low plain parapet without merlons. The walkway climbs straight ahead to a two-storey hollow brick watchtower on the near crest, left of centre. At that watchtower a small arched gate opens in the inner, right-hand side of the Wall. From it a narrow spur wall with its own walkway leaves the Wall at a right angle and runs to the right along a rocky saddle, dipping and climbing, until it reaches a separate, higher summit right of centre, about a kilometre away on the inner side of the Wall. Two porters with firewood on shoulder poles walk along that spur, tiny.

The beacon engine stands alone on that summit, apart from the Wall, as real Ming beacon towers stood apart from it. Around its foot is a low walled yard with stacks of firewood, a stone cistern and a small tiled guard hut. Its gate faces the spur and the Wall. The engine itself is a working machine with nobody living in it: a tapering grey brick base, then an open frame of dark timber storeys with deep bracket sets under grey tiled eaves, large bronze gear wheels turning on one vertical timber shaft, and a wooden water wheel in the base. The wheel is fed by a timber flume on trestles that comes from higher mountains further inland, behind the engine, away from the Wall; its spill falls as a thin white thread into the valley. The engine's top, high above the watchtower and just below the cloud deck, is an open crown of tall bronze vanes, one bent and one missing, around five separate iron fire bowls, all unlit. One storey is patched with newer pale planks and a bamboo scaffold clings to its left side. Two small oil lanterns, one bright and one dim, burn on its lowest gallery, the only warm light in the frame. The engine is pale and softened by distance, lighter than the dark watchtower in front of it, and it dwarfs the watchtower even though it is far behind it.

Further right, the Wall runs on over lower ridges toward the horizon, and beside it, each on its own summit on the inner side and each joined to the Wall by its own spur, stand smaller engines of the same design, the farthest only grey shapes.

Weather. A low, still deck of grey stratus covers the sky a few hundred metres above the crest, smooth underneath with soft darker folds. A narrow pale slit of cold white sky shows only at the far right horizon. No sunrise colour anywhere. Below the Wall a sea of cloud fills every valley to one level, with a flat top; the ridges, the saddle and the engine's summit stand out of it as dark islands, and on the right it spills slowly over a low saddle.
