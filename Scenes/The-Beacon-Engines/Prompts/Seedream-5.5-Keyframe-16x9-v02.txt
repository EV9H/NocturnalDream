Photograph, 16:9 landscape, taken from an adult's eye height standing on the brick walkway of the Ming Great Wall at Jinshanling, northeast of Beijing, at blue dawn before sunrise. 24mm lens, deep focus, handheld, level horizon.

The walkway runs away from the camera and climbs the ridge in a long flight of worn grey brick steps, about three metres wide, between a crenellated outer parapet with square loopholes on the left and a low inner wall on the right. About fifteen metres up the steps, a porter in a faded indigo padded cotton jacket and a cloth head wrap climbs away from the camera, back turned, two bundles of split firewood hanging from a bamboo shoulder pole. Halfway up the ridge the Wall passes through a two-storey grey brick watchtower with arched windows.

Straight ahead, where the Wall reaches the far summit about one kilometre away, the Wall runs into the foot of the beacon engine, about four hundred metres tall, taller than the mountain beneath it, built in the Wall's own materials. The watchtower in front of it is smaller than one of its storeys, and the engine's top stands high above the horizon line. Its lower third is a tapering grey brick base with arched gates and crenellations along its top, streaked dark with water stains. Above it stack eight storeys of dark weathered timber with deep bracket sets (dougong) under grey tiled eaves, each storey a little narrower than the one below, with open galleries where guards stand at the rail, so small at this distance that each is a speck no bigger than a single bracket. A wooden water wheel about sixty metres across sits in a slot in the base, fed by a timber flume on trestles that runs along the ridge from higher mountains behind; its spill falls down the slope below as a thin white cascade. Between the timber storeys, large bronze gear wheels with heavy teeth mesh around one vertical timber shaft. The top of the engine is an open crown: a ring of tall, separate bronze vanes standing upright like the slats of a lantern, with pale dawn sky showing through the gaps between them, around a wide shallow iron fire bowl, unlit. The tips of the vanes enter a thin band of cloud. The eaves near the top are black with old soot.

Beyond the engine the Wall continues to the right over lower and fainter ridges toward the horizon. On each next ridge stands another engine of the same design, each smaller with distance, the last two only grey silhouettes in the haze.

The slope below the near engine is logged bare, with stumps, and stacks of cut timber lie against the inner wall.

Light: cool blue pre-dawn sky, palest low on the right horizon, no direct sun. Soft blue light on the brick, shadows dark but with texture. Two small oil lanterns burn on the engine's lowest gallery, unequal in brightness, the only warm points in the frame.

Atmosphere: mist lies low in the valleys between ridges, so each ridge separates from the next in a lighter value. The air along the Wall is clear.

Palette: slate-blue sky, grey brick, ochre dry autumn grass, dark timber and bronze with green patina in the recesses.

Materials: grey Ming bricks with rounded, worn edges and lime mortar, moss and lichen in the joints, the centre of each step tread hollowed by feet, a few replaced bricks lighter in tone, dark wet brick under the flume where it drips.
