Fine-art large-format colour photograph, 4x5 view camera on a tripod, vertical 3:4, made from high on the canyon bank well back from Hukou Waterfall on the Yellow River. The camera is level and still, deadpan, with the horizon high and straight.

The picture is mostly pale. A milky, overexposed white sky fills the top third with no detail. The loess canyon walls and the wide flat sandstone shelves are washed-out cream, sand and dusty pink, low in contrast, like a faded museum print.

Through the middle of the frame the whole river pours over the broken lip of the falls into its narrow stone trough as perfectly clear, cold jade-green water. It is the only saturated colour in the picture: one green ribbon cut into yellow rock, with a thin white seam of spray where it drops. Dried yellow silt still stains the rock above the waterline.

On the bare sandstone shelf beside the falls, very small in the lower third, one middle-aged man in a dark padded coat stands side-on, looking down at a red plastic bucket of clear water at his feet. Nothing else around him: wide empty rock, a few dry weeds, one loose rope lying on the stone.

Soft, shadowless overcast light. Extreme fine detail across the whole frame, everything sharp from near rock to far ridge. Gentle colour-negative film grain, slightly lifted blacks, muted pastel palette with the jade green and the small red bucket as the only accents. Quiet, melancholic, a little absurd. No text, no signs.
