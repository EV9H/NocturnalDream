Photograph, vertical 3:4. Reference: Gregory Crewdson's staged suburban stillness with Benoit Debie's two opposing colours of light.

Camera: standing on the flat concrete roof of an old six-storey apartment block, shooting from behind three teenagers at chest height, tilted slightly upward and a little crooked. The nearest roof ledge and a rusted water tank are soft and out of focus in the left foreground.

Scene: a summer night during a citywide blackout. Every apartment tower and street around is completely dark, no lit windows anywhere, the skyline only a black silhouette. Above it the Milky Way is impossibly bright and dense, bright enough that the teenagers cast faint soft shadows across the roof. Three teenagers sit on the ledge seen from behind, one pointing up, one holding a lit candle in a glass jar beside her on the concrete. Laundry lines, a satellite dish, potted plants in old paint tins, a folded bamboo mat.

Light: two sources only. Cold ink-blue starlight from above lights the roof, shoulders and laundry and makes the faint shadows. Everything else falls to deep blue-black, about 70 percent of the frame in darkness.

Mood: the whole city has gone quiet and for once you can see the sky. Tender childhood wonder with a small chill of strangeness.
