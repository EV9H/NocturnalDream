Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: contemporary 35mm film, Leica M6 with a 35mm f/2 lens wide open, CineStill 800T tungsten film shot at night: strong red halation around every bright light, cool teal shadows, visible grain, shallow focus, in the manner of Todd Hido's House Hunting photographs taken through a car window.

Camera: handheld from the same window seat near the back of an almost empty night bus, at a sitting passenger's eye height, angled slightly forward through the window glass, framing a little crooked. Focus is on the scene outside; raindrops on the glass in the foreground are soft circles of light, and a faint fog of condensation clouds the lower corner of the pane. The bus interior is reflected faintly over the view: a row of empty seats, a yellow handrail, the fluorescent ceiling strip. The bus is moving slowly, so the nearest streetlight is slightly smeared sideways.

Scene: a sleeping suburban street at about 3 am, seen in passing. Low fog hangs between the houses. In the middle distance stands one ordinary detached two-storey house with a pitched roof, a small front lawn and a low hedge; every window is dark except one upstairs window, lit warm yellow behind a thin curtain. A single sodium streetlight stands at the kerb in front of it, its orange cone of light fading into the fog. A parked hatchback sits under the light with dew on its roof. The wet road and pavement shine. The other houses fade into grey fog and darkness. No people anywhere. Nothing impossible: this is an ordinary street.

Light: the sodium streetlight is the main source, orange, from upper left of the house, blooming red at its edges; the one warm upstairs window; the cold greenish fluorescent of the bus interior only in the reflection on the glass. The sky, the gaps between houses and the lower windows stay dark.

Mood: passing a house where one person is still awake, and not knowing whose it is or where the bus is going now. Lonely, tender, quiet, like a memory you are not sure is yours.

Avoid: readable text, house numbers, logos, people's faces, horror cues, modern glass architecture, cinematic grading, HDR.
