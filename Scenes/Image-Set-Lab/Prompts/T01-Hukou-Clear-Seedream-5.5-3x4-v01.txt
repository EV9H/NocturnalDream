Vertical 3:4 photograph taken at adult eye height from the rocky Shanxi-side bank of Hukou Waterfall on the Yellow River, 40mm lens, looking down the falls into the narrow stone trough below. The whole Yellow River drops over the broken lip of the falls here and pours into the trough, but today the water is completely clear: glass-green and deep jade where it is thick, so clear at the edges that the ribbed grey-brown sandstone riverbed shows through the shallows. White foam and spray churn at the base of the drop; the spray is the only thing in the air.

The banks are unchanged and still carry the river's usual colour: bands of dried yellow silt and mud stains coat the layered sandstone above the waterline, a wet ochre high-water line runs along the rock, and in one slack eddy near the bottom left a small pocket of water is still faintly cloudy yellow. Beyond the falls, the yellow loess canyon walls of the Shanxi–Shaanxi gorge rise on both sides with terraced slopes and sparse winter scrub.

Foreground: weathered, pitted sandstone ledges, a puddle holding clear water, a rusted iron safety railing post with flaking paint at the right edge. Midground: the falls and the green tongue of water curving into the trough. Background: the gorge receding upstream under a pale sky.

Light: flat high overcast stratus, soft and even, no sun, so the water's true colour reads; slightly darker clouds upstream. Distant rock is lower in contrast and a little bluish; near rock is crisp and darker. Palette of loess ochre, wet grey stone and cold jade green, with the green as the only saturated colour.

Unpolished documentary feel: Kodak Portra 400 film scan, fine grain, soft highlight roll-off on the spray, slight vignette, a little uneven exposure, horizon very slightly tilted. Keep the bottom fifth of the frame to rock and railing. No people, no text, no signs.
