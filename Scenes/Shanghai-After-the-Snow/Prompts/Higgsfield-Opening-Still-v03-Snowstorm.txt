One immersive vertical 9:16 low-light photograph from an adult pedestrian's eye height on Shanghai's west-bank Bund promenade. Last dark minutes before dawn, in the middle of a heavy, wind-driven snowstorm. The camera is level and faces square across the Huangpu toward Pudong. The Bund railing runs horizontally across the lower half of the frame and its posts stand vertical.

The entire promenade, edge to edge, is buried under continuous deep, soft snow. In the foreground, overlapping trails of bootprints from several earlier pedestrians cross the snow left to right. Some have distinct compressed soles and raised rims, and older tracks have partly filled in with fresh powder. Even inside the prints the ground is snow. At the river edge a continuous, wind-shaped bank piles up nearly to the waist-high top rail, varying in height gradually across the frame. Heavy rounded caps of snow sit all along the top rail and on every post. Only small portions of dark wave-pattern steel peek through in a few places. The drift and its snow cover continue across the post joints.

Make the storm actively present in the photograph. Thick flurries sweep diagonally from upper right to lower left at several depths, with a few soft near-lens flakes, sharper lit flakes in the middle distance and veils of blowing snow sweeping low over the promenade and river. Beyond the rail, the wide Huangpu is dark open water with short broken reflections. Across it, the same storm has left distinctly pale snow caps on low flat Pudong roofs and stepped terraces, and a continuous snowy strip along the far-bank riverwalk. Those surfaces are clearly visible through gaps in the blowing snow. Behind them, the Oriental Pearl's spheres and spire, Shanghai Tower and the World Financial Center appear at credible distance through the storm, with uneven clusters of lit offices.
