Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, printed warm. Heavy grain in the shadows, warm sodium-gold and cream tones, deep blacks that still hold a faint trace of texture, highlights softly blooming with halation. The same old grand hotel as the rest of the set: peeling plaster, worn gilt, spotted mirrors, parquet, white tablecloths.

Camera: 43mm lens, tripod at standing eye height at the dark back of the hotel foyer, looking past a marble column in the soft foreground toward the hotel's tall glass front doors, which stand open onto the night street. Long exposure of about 2 seconds.

Scene: 3 am. The foyer is empty and dark: a worn carpet, a reception desk in shadow, a coat stand with nothing on it. Behind the camera, the ballroom's light is going out; only a last warm stripe of it falls across the foyer floor toward the doors.

Light with emphasis: a single source only. About seventy percent of the frame falls into deep darkness; the light lands on the subject and nowhere else, with a steep falloff. No fill light, no evenly lit walls, no bright ceiling. Light: one old street lamp outside the open doors, warm and blooming through the wet glass, rim-lighting the two figures; the last stripe of warm light on the foyer floor; everything else is darkness.
