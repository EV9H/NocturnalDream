Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, printed warm. Heavy grain in the shadows, warm sodium-gold and cream tones, deep blacks that still hold a faint trace of texture, highlights softly blooming with halation. The same old grand hotel as the rest of the set: peeling plaster, worn gilt, spotted mirrors, parquet, white tablecloths.

Camera: 80mm lens, tripod at standing chest height in a dark side corridor of the ballroom, aimed slightly off to the left at a tall antique gilt mirror on the wall, framed off-centre so the mirror fills most of the frame with a strip of dark wall and a sconce at the right edge. Long exposure of about 4 seconds.

Scene: 3 am. The corridor in front of the mirror is completely empty: worn carpet, a velvet bench, nobody. The old mirror glass is spotted and clouded with silver loss. In the reflection, the ballroom behind the camera is visible, and in it a couple is still slow-dancing, blurred into soft translucent motion by the long exposure, close together, her pale dress swinging. Nobody stands in front of the mirror; they exist only in the reflection.

Light with emphasis: a single source only. About seventy percent of the frame falls into deep darkness; the light lands on the subject and nowhere else, with a steep falloff. No fill light, no evenly lit walls, no bright ceiling. Light: one small wall sconce at the right edge with a fringed shade, warm, lighting the gilt frame and the mirror; the reflected couple glows faintly from a distant light inside the reflection; everything else is black.

Mood: the room is empty but the mirror still remembers them. Haunted, tender, quiet.

Avoid: evenly lit room, bright walls or ceiling, multiple light sources, spotlights, faces, readable text, logos, horror cues, clean modern interiors, cinematic grading, HDR.
