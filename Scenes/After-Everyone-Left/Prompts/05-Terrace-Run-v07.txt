Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, printed warm. Heavy grain in the shadows, warm sodium-gold and cream tones, deep blacks that still hold a faint trace of texture, highlights softly blooming with halation. The same old grand hotel as the rest of the set: peeling plaster, worn gilt, spotted mirrors, parquet, white tablecloths.

Camera: 43mm lens, tripod inside the dark ballroom, a few steps back from a tall arched window, framing the window off-centre so the black window frame and a heavy curtain fill the left and lower foreground. Raindrops and streaks on the glass are in soft focus. Long exposure of about 1 second.

Scene: 3 am, rain. Inside, nothing is visible but the dark curtain edge and the silhouette of the window frame. Outside, on the stone terrace with a balustrade, under a single old iron lamp, the couple is running together through the rain in the same direction, side by side and pressed close, from left to right across the terrace. There is no umbrella. The man has taken off his dark suit jacket and holds it up with both hands, stretched open like a little roof, over both of their heads, tilted toward her so that she is under the middle of it and his own back and shoulder are out in the rain, his white shirt soaked through. She is tucked under the jacket against his side, barefoot in a pale evening dress, her shoes in one hand, laughing up at him. They are small in the upper right third of the frame, mid-stride, and the long exposure smears their running into soft translucent streaks; rain splashes up from the wet stone that reflects the lamp.

Light with emphasis: a single source only. About seventy percent of the frame falls into deep darkness; the light lands on the subject and nowhere else, with a steep falloff. No fill light, no evenly lit walls, no bright ceiling. Light: the terrace lamp outside is the only light, warm, with the rain streaking in its glow; the interior is black.
