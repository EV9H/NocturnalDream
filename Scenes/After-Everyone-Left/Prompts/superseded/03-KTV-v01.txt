Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7 with a 43mm wide lens, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, long exposure on a tripod (about 4 seconds), printed warm. Heavy grain in the shadows, warm sodium-gold tones, deep crushed blacks, highlights softly blooming with halation. Reference: Hiroshi Sugimoto's long exposures and Gregory Crewdson's staged stillness.

Camera: tripod at waist height in the doorway of a small karaoke (KTV) room, level and centred, looking across the low table toward the screen and the long sofa.

Scene: 4 am, the booking long over. A U-shaped faux-leather sofa, a glass table crowded with empty beer bottles, a fruit plate gone brown, crumpled tissues, two dice cups, a tambourine. The screen on the wall shows only a soft blue idle glow with no readable words. A small mirror ball turns slowly, and its specks of light are drawn into short curved streaks across the walls by the long exposure. Standing in front of the screen, one person is still singing into a microphone, their body and free arm a soft translucent blur; the microphone cable is the only sharp part. Everyone else has gone; jackets are gone from the sofa.

Light: the screen's cool blue from the front, the warm low ceiling lamp, the mirror-ball streaks; corners stay dark.

Mood: the last song nobody else stayed for. Lonely, warm, a little funny, a little sad.

Avoid: horror cues, faces, readable text, logos, clean modern interiors, cinematic grading, HDR.
