Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7 with a 43mm wide lens, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, long exposure on a tripod (about 4 seconds), printed warm. Palette is almost monochrome amber: warm sodium-gold and tungsten tones only, no blue or green anywhere, deep crushed blacks, heavy grain in the shadows, the main light softly blooming with halation.

Camera: tripod at waist height, perfectly level, strict one-point perspective centred on the room's axis, symmetrical, the polished floor filling the lower half of the frame and reflecting the light.

Position: at the entrance of an old-fashioned grand banquet hall of a Chinese restaurant (酒楼), looking straight down the central aisle between the tables toward a small stage with a plain red backdrop.

Scene: 3 am after a wedding banquet. Round tables on both sides with white cloths, chairs pushed back, empty glasses and lazy Susans, fallen red paper confetti and ribbons on the carpeted aisle, gilded columns, mirrored panels on the walls. In the aisle under the chandelier, two people in their good clothes are still dancing slowly together. Because of the long exposure, the people are soft translucent motion-blurred ghosts, smeared by their movement, only their feet slightly sharper; everything else in the room is perfectly still and sharp. Dust hangs in the air in the light.

Light: one warm overhead source at the centre of the room is the only real light, pooling gold on the floor beneath it and falling off into darkness toward the walls, corners and ceiling; a few dim wall sconces glow faintly.

Mood: the guests have gone home, and two of them stayed for one more song. Warm, haunted, tender.

Avoid: horror cues, faces, readable text, logos, clean modern interiors, cool or blue light, cinematic grading, HDR.
