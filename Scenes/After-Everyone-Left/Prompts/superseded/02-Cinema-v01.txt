Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7 with a 43mm wide lens, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, long exposure on a tripod (about 4 seconds), printed warm. Heavy grain in the shadows, warm sodium-gold tones, deep crushed blacks, highlights softly blooming with halation. Reference: Hiroshi Sugimoto's long exposures and Gregory Crewdson's staged stillness.

Camera: tripod at seated eye height in the back row of an old single-screen cinema, level and centred, looking down the aisle toward the screen.

Scene: just after 2 am, long after the last show. The projector is still running, so the screen is a pure glowing white rectangle (the long exposure has burned the film to white). Rows of worn red velvet seats, several flipped up, popcorn boxes and a paper cup on the sticky carpet, an aisle light strip along the steps, a green exit glow off to one side, out of focus. In the middle of the middle row, one couple is still sitting close together, her head on his shoulder. Because of the long exposure they are soft, translucent and slightly smeared, as if they shifted once during the exposure; every other seat is empty and sharp.

Light: the white screen is the only strong source, lighting the seat backs and the couple from the front, falling off to darkness at the sides and ceiling; the warm aisle strip at floor level.

Mood: the film ended and nobody told them. Tender, still, half-remembered.

Avoid: horror cues, faces, readable text, logos, clean modern interiors, cinematic grading, HDR.
