Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7 with a 43mm wide lens, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, long exposure on a tripod (about 4 seconds), printed warm. Heavy grain in the shadows, warm sodium-gold tones, deep crushed blacks, highlights softly blooming with halation. Reference: Hiroshi Sugimoto's long exposures and Gregory Crewdson's staged stillness.

Camera: tripod at waist height at the rail of an old indoor roller rink, level, looking across the wooden rink floor toward the far wall.

Scene: closing time, 1 am. Half the lights are already off. The polished maple rink floor shines; a still mirror ball hangs in the middle; rows of rental skates sit in cubbies by the counter, and the snack bar shutter is down. One skater is still going round and round. Because of the long exposure they appear as a single soft translucent figure near the far curve, trailed by a faint ghostly arc of themselves around the oval, fading out behind them. The rest of the rink is completely empty.

Light: one bank of warm overhead lights on over the far half of the floor, a few coloured spots dimmed to a glow, the mirror ball catching small points of light; the near half and the ceiling are dark.

Mood: one more lap before the lights go out. Nostalgic, lonely, gentle.

Avoid: horror cues, faces, readable text, logos, clean modern interiors, cinematic grading, HDR.
