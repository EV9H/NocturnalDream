Photograph, vertical 3:4. Look and era: 1990s fine-art night photography on a medium-format Mamiya 7 with a 43mm wide lens, Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop, long exposure on a tripod (about 4 seconds), printed warm. Heavy grain in the shadows, warm sodium-gold tones, deep crushed blacks, highlights softly blooming with halation. Reference: Hiroshi Sugimoto's long exposures and Gregory Crewdson's staged stillness.

Camera: tripod at seated height just inside the door of a small all-night noodle shop, level and centred, looking down the narrow room toward the open kitchen pass.

Scene: about 5 am, after closing. Chairs are upturned on most of the tables; the floor is still wet from mopping and reflects the light; a mop bucket stands by the counter; steam rises faintly from a big stockpot left on low in the kitchen. At the very last table, under the only light still on, two people are still sitting across from each other over two bowls, leaning in. Because of the long exposure they are soft and translucent, their hands a gentle blur; the bowls, chopsticks and the steam are sharp.

Light: one warm pendant lamp over the last table, the cooler fluorescent spill from the kitchen pass, a faint blue dawn at the front window behind the camera reflecting on the wet floor. The rest stays dark.

Mood: nobody wanted to be the first to say goodnight. Tender, warm, slightly unreal.

Avoid: horror cues, faces, readable text, logos, clean modern interiors, cinematic grading, HDR.
