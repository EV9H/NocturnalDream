# The Last Afternoon / 最后一节课

**SC-009 (provisional) · Brief v0.1 · Stage 1 scene · Production: proposed, creative approval pending · Publication: unscheduled**

Working copy of the [video production checklist v1.2](../../Studio-Reference/VIDEO_PRODUCTION_TEMPLATE.md). Proposed format is one 9:16 shot, roughly 12 seconds to give the room time to register. Model, settings and budget are pending.

## 1. Scene brief

**Familiar reality.** The last school afternoon in a mainland Chinese secondary-school classroom. For viewers who remember it, establish the place with the chalk timetable still at the edge of the blackboard and rows of scratched wood-and-steel desks with stools tucked underneath. The worn terrazzo corridor and the wall-mounted school PA speaker support the same memory.

**Alteration.** The school is dark for the night. In one empty classroom, the late-afternoon sun is still coming through its window. The far window looks onto the same campus at that hour. The school architecture and objects hold their positions. The classroom's light spills naturally across the threshold and weakens on the corridor floor.

**Discovery and emotional recognition.** Begin beside the corridor parapet, with the opposite wing dark beyond it. Move along the corridor until a strip of sunlight on the terrazzo leads the eye through the open door. See the empty desks before reading the window and the afternoon outside it. Hold on the long patch of sun across the window-seat desk and its empty stool. The intended feeling is the ache of coming back to a place that still feels like yours, after the people who made it yours have gone.

**STEPPS hypotheses.** Emotion comes from the hold on the empty seat in remembered light. Triggers come from the wall speaker's brief end-of-class tone and familiar classroom furniture. Use an original tone with the narrow speaker sound of a campus PA. Social Currency, Public, Practical Value and Stories were considered. Audience response is unmeasured.

**Reference needs.** Choose a consistent school era and campus typology. Source a corridor/door reference for geometry, a classroom reference for fixtures and furniture, and a late-afternoon room reference for light. Record source and rights when selected. The setting is fictional.

## 2. Art direction and shot plan

**Velvet Afterimage with Midnight Witness.** One slow eye-level lateral move past the open door, at roughly 35 mm equivalent. Keep an edge of the dark corridor in frame at the end. Cool open-air night fill keeps the near terrazzo and doorframe readable. The main source is low amber sunlight from the classroom window, raking across desks and falling off across the corridor floor. Fine dust is visible only where it crosses the beam. Desks, edges and chalk residue hold their texture. The time difference is the single altered condition. Shadows and spill obey the window's direction throughout.

**Sound.** Use the open corridor's night air and faint off-campus traffic. As the doorway enters view, one distant school bell has the thin, slightly clipped sound of an old PA speaker, followed by its short room decay. Keep the classroom quiet after the tone. No score or voiceover is planned.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Corridor | Parapet, terrazzo seams, doorframe and opposite wing | Perspective shifts with the lateral move | Opening, door reveal and hold |
| Classroom | Clear desk rows, chalkboard and window-seat desk | Changes in visibility from the doorway | Reveal and hold |

**First test.** After approval, compose one still at the end of the move. At phone size, can a viewer read the actual night outside the corridor, afternoon through the classroom window, and the vacant desk in one coherent view? If the image reads as ordinary artificial light, clarify the directional shadows and the lit campus exterior before testing the camera move.

## Other directions considered

- **城门听雨 / Rain at the Water Gate.** In a steampunk reimagining of a Jiangnan canal town, a huge steam-driven mechanism lifts an old floodgate past worn stone steps and moored boats. Water and rain stay physically credible. Emotion is the quiet relief of seeing the water held back. Social Currency is seeing a familiar canal town's relationship with water expressed through its own architecture and an imagined engineering history.
- **梅雨天的弄堂 / The Lane After the Rain.** In a Shanghai lane, a puddle under the shared eaves reflects a clear summer afternoon while the buildings above are still in nighttime rain. Laundry poles cross both views, tying the altered reflection to a familiar place. Emotion is the brief warmth of recognizing the lane on a better day. Triggers are the everyday sounds of rain off the eaves.

**Concept rank.** The Last Afternoon first for the direct link between the alteration and a lived school memory. Rain at the Water Gate second for cultural specificity and scale. The Lane After the Rain third for using a more familiar visual mechanism. The Last Afternoon is also the simplest first test. The Lane After the Rain needs reliable reflected geometry. Rain at the Water Gate needs a larger environment and credible machine-and-water interactions.
