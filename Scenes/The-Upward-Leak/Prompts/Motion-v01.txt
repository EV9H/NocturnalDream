One continuous six-second vertical 9:16 locked tripod shot, low moderate-wide view of a small old municipal pump room. In the upper third, a horizontal cast-iron pipe with a rusted coupling and one dull burgundy valve wheel on the right. In the lower third, a palm-sized shallow silver puddle on dark worn tile, directly below a pinhole on the pipe's underside. A fixed drain grate sits to the puddle's left. Hold both endpoints sharp and visible with clear air between them. A hard silver shaft of light from a high window camera-left grazes the gap, faint dust, readable charcoal shadows, worn ivory tile grout. Restrained photographic realism.

First second: still room, puddle and pipe already in view. At about one second, the middle of the puddle dimples and bulges into one bright grape-sized water bead. The bead detaches visibly from the floor and rises straight up through the lit air, with a crisp silver edge. Hold its upward travel long enough to read. Around four seconds it touches the underside of the pipe and is absorbed into the pinhole. Hold on a small wet glint at the pipe as the puddle's ripples settle. The bead is the only object that moves through the air. Keep the camera fixed and the cast iron, valve, grout and drain unchanged throughout.
