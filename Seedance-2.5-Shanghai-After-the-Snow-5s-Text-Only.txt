NOCTURNAL DREAM / SHANGHAI AFTER THE SNOW
Independent visual scene. One continuous five-second vertical 9:16 take.

CONCEPT & SCALE
In the last dark minutes before dawn, Shanghai’s familiar waterfront is almost waist-deep in snow. The storm has passed. The city is still there across the water, but the walkway feels newly unfamiliar. A partially buried railing, worn paving and old footprints give the snow a human scale.

WORLD DESIGN & VISUAL DISCOVERY
Walk along the west-bank Bund promenade with its metal guardrail and the open Huangpu River to the left. Look obliquely across the water toward Lujiazui. The Oriental Pearl’s spheres, the World Financial Center’s trapezoidal opening and the taper of Shanghai Tower identify the city through the haze. Start beside an irregular shoulder of snow. As the walker passes it, reveal a longer stretch of cleared path and more open water through the rail. Give the frame generous dark space above the skyline.

Snow drifts reach roughly one metre in sheltered pockets and taper across exposed paving. The cleared route is walkable, with compacted layers, slumped banks, scuffed footprints and a few flecks of dirty ice where snow meets stone. A brief ground-level gust lifts loose powder and lets it settle. The river stays open, with small ripples breaking the reflections.

ART DIRECTION & LIGHT — MIDNIGHT WITNESS
Midnight blue is the dominant color. Charcoal rail, stone and building shadows retain distinct layers. Cool silver-gray snow catches soft overcast skylight. A single small amber practical off-frame to the inland side and slightly behind the walker grazes one bank, revealing its texture through a narrow edge of warm light. Its falloff leaves most of the snow cool. A few distant windows give the far shore scale. Let thin haze gather over the water while the nearest snow and railing stay tactile. Keep the horizon dark, the highlights soft and exposure consistent. Fine low-light grain, restrained halation at distant points of light, no orange horizon or broad amber wash.

CAMERA & MOTION
Begin in stride at human eye height. Walk forward approximately three metres beside the railing at a measured pace. A nearby railing post passes out of the frame and the next segment comes into view. The camera’s natural weight shifts and small changes in gaze carry the entire composition, including the far shore and skyline. The foreground sweeps past, the opposite shoreline slides across the frame, and the towers drift with the changing perspective. Render their actual depth and evolving overlaps, with buildings holding their physical shape and position in the city. Use a fixed focal length and natural motion blur. Keep moving through the last frame.

SOUND / SCALE
If audio is generated, use close packed-snow footfalls, a brief puff of wind, low river wash and faint city ambience from the far bank. No music or narration.

CONTINUITY
Keep the rail anchored, the walkway continuous, the snow settled and the towers solid. Avoid camera zoom, frozen backdrop, architectural morphing, snow canyon, heavy falling snow, neon spill, glossy HDR and cuts.
